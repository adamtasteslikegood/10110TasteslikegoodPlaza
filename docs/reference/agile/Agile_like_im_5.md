# Explain "Agile" to a 5yr old:

> **Authority: none.** Tier 4 `research`, the plain-language companion to
> [`Agile_Expaiined.md`](Agile_Expaiined.md). Authoritative over nothing.

Imagine you want to build a giant LEGO castle.

The old way (Waterfall): You draw the whole castle on paper first. Every tower, every window. Then you build it for six months without stopping. At the end you show your mom — and she says “oh, I wanted a spaceship.” Too late. All that work, wrong thing.

Agile: You build a little piece — just one tower — in two weeks. You show mom. She says “cool, but can it be blue?” You fix it. Then you build the next piece. Show her again. Every two weeks you have something real she can look at and hold.

The core ideas:

- Sprints — short chunks of time (usually 2 weeks) where you build one small working piece
- Standup — every morning everyone says: what I did, what I’m doing, what’s stuck
- Backlog — the big list of everything you might build, sorted by what matters most
- Retro — after each sprint, the team asks “what sucked? let’s do it differently”

The whole bet is that you can’t know what you want until you see something real. So build small, show often, change your mind cheaply.

The tradeoff people don’t mention: it’s terrible at answering “when will the whole castle be done?” You trade long-term predictability for the ability to be wrong without it costing you six months.

## Version 2:


Imagine you’re building a LEGO castle with friends.

The old way (waterfall): You draw the whole castle on paper first, spend six months building it exactly like the drawing, then show your mom — and she says “oh, I wanted a spaceship.”

Agile: You build a little tower in two weeks. Show mom. She says “cool, but add a door.” You build the door in the next two weeks. Show her again. Keep going, small chunk by small chunk, always showing your work and asking “still good?”

The rules of the game:

- Sprints — short chunks of time (usually 2 weeks) where you build one small thing
- Standup — every morning everyone says what they built yesterday, what they’re building today, and what’s stuck
- Backlog — the wish list of everything you might build, sorted by what matters most
- Retro — after each chunk, you all talk about what went well and what sucked, so the next chunk goes better

The whole point: it’s easier to change direction after two weeks than after two years. You find out you’re building the wrong thing early, when it’s cheap to fix.
