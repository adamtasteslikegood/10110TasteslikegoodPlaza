Project management for software development

Agile

You cannot fully specify software before you build it. So you stop making one long bet, and start making many short ones.

01 — the structural difference

The whole thing is about feedback latency

Waterfall and Agile build the same software with the same people. What differs is how long you go before finding out you were wrong — and therefore how much it costs to fix.

The premise

Requirements change, users discover what they want by using things, and estimates degrade the further out you look. None of that is a discipline problem — it is the nature of the work.

The consequence

If you cannot know the answer in advance, optimise for finding out fast. Every practice in Agile is downstream of that single move.

03 — scrum

Three roles, three artifacts, one loop

Scrum is a time-boxed implementation of the values. Work happens in fixed-length Sprints, usually one or two weeks.

Product Owner

what & why

Owns and orders the backlog. Single wringable neck for value delivered. A PO without real decision authority is the most common way Scrum quietly fails.

Scrum Master

process

Removes blockers and protects the sprint from mid-flight scope injection. Not a manager, not a secretary, not a status collector.

Development Team

how

Self-organising and cross-functional, ideally 3–9 people. Decides how work gets built and commits to what fits in the sprint.

Product Backlog

Everything that might get built, ordered by value. Living — always re-prioritised, never frozen.

Sprint Backlog

The slice committed to this sprint. Frozen once the sprint starts.

Increment

The shippable output at sprint end. Must meet the Definition of Done.

The sprint loop

Four ceremonies, four timeboxes

Sprint PlanningStart of sprint2–4 hrsDecide what is in, break it into tasks, agree a sprint goal.

04 — the work

Big things decompose into shippable things

Each level exists so the level below it can be estimated. If a story cannot be sized, it is still an epic.

Initiativequarters

Strategic outcome. Never built directly — it gives epics a reason to exist.

Epicweeks–months

A body of work too big to finish inside one sprint.

User Storydays

A slice of user-visible value — sized, testable, shippable on its own.

Taskhours

How a story gets built. Written and owned by the developers.

Template

As a <type of user>
I want <some capability>
So that <some benefit>

Plus acceptance criteria — the testable conditions that define done. Without AC, a story is a wish, and "done" becomes a matter of opinion at review time.

How a story actually moves

Backlog→ Refined→ Committed→ In Progress→ In Review→ Testing→ Done ·Blocked

REWORK LOOPS

In Review sends work back on changes requested. Testing sends it back on bugs found. Both loops are normal; both are invisible on a plan that only counts forward motion.

Blocked is the state that matters

A story sitting in Blocked is the single most useful signal a standup produces. If nothing is ever blocked, people are working around problems silently.

06 — estimating

Points measure size, not hours

Points bundle complexity, uncertainty and effort into one relative number. Humans are bad at absolute duration estimates and reliably good at comparative ones.

Planning poker

Estimate this story privately, then reveal with the team: "Let users reset their password by email link."

Pick a card to reveal the team.

Velocity is not a performance metric

It is a forecasting tool the team uses on itself. The moment velocity becomes a target, estimates inflate and the number stops meaning anything. This is Goodhart's Law, and it happens with near-total reliability.

07 — engineering practices

Without these, Agile is just short waterfalls with more meetings

Most of these come from Extreme Programming. They are what make a two-week feedback loop physically possible rather than aspirational.

Continuous integration

Merge to main frequently. Automated build and test on every push, and a red pipeline stops the line.

Automated testing

You cannot iterate quickly on code you are afraid to change. Tests buy the courage to refactor.

Continuous refactoring

Small, safe, constant — not a "refactoring sprint" scheduled for never.

Short-lived branches

Long branches recreate the integration problem Agile was invented to solve.

Feature flags

Decouple deploy from release so unfinished work can ship dark and be switched on later.

Definition of Done

Explicit and shared: reviewed, tested, merged, deployable. Otherwise done is an opinion.

The common root cause

Almost every item above is one failure wearing a different hat: the organisation kept its old decision-making structure and asked only the development team to change. Agile is a change to who decides what, and when — if that does not move, nothing does.

09 — limits

When Agile is the wrong choice

Agile assumes requirements are uncertain and iteration is cheap. Where neither holds, it fits badly — and saying so is not heresy.

Safety-critical & regulated

avoid

Avionics, medical devices, anything where upfront verification is a legal requirement rather than a preference.

Fixed-scope fixed-price

avoid

The contract structure fights the methodology. You cannot welcome changing requirements when scope is the payment term.

Genuinely understood work

avoid

A migration with a known destination does not need iterative discovery. It needs a plan and execution.

Solo or two-person work

avoid

Most of the ceremony is overhead for coordination you do not actually have.

On scaling: LeSS and Nexus add little to Scrum beyond a shared backlog and joint planning. SAFe is heavyweight and widely criticised for reintroducing the bureaucracy Agile was reacting against — though it is sometimes what a 500-person org genuinely needs. The Spotify model was a snapshot of one moment; Spotify itself does not run it as described. Scaling frameworks solve coordination. If the real problem is unclear ownership or bad architecture, no framework fixes it — Conway's Law wins either way.

In one sentence

Ship small slices of working software frequently, show them to real users, and let what you learn reorder what you build next.

What most teams get wrong

They adopt the ceremonies and skip the engineering practices, then wonder why iterating still hurts.

What to do on Monday

Pick one loop and shorten it. Cut WIP, or write acceptance criteria before work starts, or make the retro produce exactly one owned action. One change, measured.
